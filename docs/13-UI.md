# 13 · The visual system

Typography, colour, density, motion, accessibility and internationalisation. The **interface
architecture** — screens, navigation, keyboard — is [19-INTERFACE](19-INTERFACE.md). Authoring
tools are [21-AUTHORING](21-AUTHORING.md). The assistant's surfaces are
[23-ASSISTANT-UI](23-ASSISTANT-UI.md).

---

## 1 · The design problem, stated exactly

One system has to serve five kinds of content that normally get five different systems:

| Content | Wants |
|---|---|
| Reading prose — write-ups, reasons, notes | a 72-character measure, generous line height |
| Dense typed rows — 500 obligations with amounts and dates | tight rows, tabular figures, aligned columns, the full width |
| Small UI labels | legibility at 11–12px |
| Spoken identifiers — `#125`, `D41`, `SG23` | monospaced, unmistakable, never wrapped |
| Arabic beside Latin | a larger optical size, more leading, and no letter-spacing |

Plus: dark and light both first-class, a contrast gate strict enough to refuse a theme, and a rule
that **no component may write a colour**.

## 2 · Colour, as roles

### 2.1 · The role list

Roles, not values. A theme maps them; a component reads them; neither may name a hex.

| Group | Roles |
|---|---|
| Surfaces, ascending | `sunk` · `bg` · `s1` · `s2` · `s3` |
| Ink, descending | `ink` · `ink2` · `dim` · `faint` |
| Boundaries | `line` (decorative hairline) · **`edge`** (load-bearing, ≥3:1) |
| Intent (colour roles — the four author words are in §2.3) | `earn` · `warn` · `danger`, each with a `-fill` variant solved ≥ 3:1 for chips and bars; `info` resolves to `accent` |
| Interactive | `accent` (the fill) · `accent-text` · `accent-ink` · `focus-in` · `focus-out` |

This is the whole list, and [28-THEMING](28-THEMING.md) §3 generates exactly it. An earlier draft
also named `line2`, `line3`, `good-value`, `good-state` and `info` as roles; none is produced by the
generator, and the tone words were collapsed to four in §2.3.

Depth comes from the **surface ladder**, which is deterministic and testable — never from
translucency. Blur behind a column of small tabular digits is a measurable legibility cost with no
information gain, and it puts every figure's contrast at the mercy of whatever scrolls under it.

> **And there is exactly one environment where the ladder turns against itself.** Under
> `forced-colors: active` the user agent overrides every colour property and **`box-shadow`
> computes to `none`** — so `sunk`, `bg`, `s1`, `s2` and `s3` all become `Canvas`, a five-level
> ladder becomes one level, and every block boundary in the product disappears. The elevation
> ladder cannot rescue it, because the shadows are gone too. The fix is a `@layer a11y` block that
> re-expresses each boundary as a border, and a validator check that asserts it —
> [28-THEMING](28-THEMING.md) §2.5 and check **L**. This was an unnoticed gap: the best structural
> decision in the visual system had one mode in which it produced a flat grey page.

### 2.1b · Identity is a third axis — one mark and one accent per thing

The predecessor's rule is *"colour means which project, everywhere"*: one accent per entity,
generated in OKLCH, validated distinct in both themes, **never hand-picked**, and never the only
signal — the name always sits beside it. §2.3 closes intent at four colours and
[28-THEMING](28-THEMING.md) refuses per-page themes. **Identity is neither**: it is not an intent and
it is not a theme, and without saying so the four-intent rule would forbid a product having a colour.

```yaml
register: entities
identity: { mark: mark, accent: hue }       # a register may declare its rows HAVE an identity
```

| An accent may colour | An accent may **never** colour |
|---|---|
| the row's mark, chip and avatar · its calendar dot · its series on a chart | text · an intent · a surface · any border that carries meaning |

The hue is **chosen at create and frozen** — an `int` field written once
([ADR-028](16-DECISIONS.md)), never a `derive:` combinator; an earlier draft wrote
`derive: { hue_from: id }`, which is not in the closed list of seven. The kernel never computes it: at
create it calls the injected `accent(existingHues)`, which `record` wires to the theme's
`nextAccentHue` — a deterministic farthest-point pick on the hue circle from every existing accent
and from the three intent hues, so a mark never reads as an intent ([28-THEMING](28-THEMING.md);
[39-SPEC-THEME](39-SPEC-THEME.md) §5). The validator then holds it ≥ 3:1 against every surface and
distinct from every other accent under all three dichromat simulations — four marks at ΔE ≥ 4, and
past four the name carries identity; a golden-angle sequence in the kernel was considered and
withdrawn. It reaches the DOM as `oklch(L C h)` with the theme's two mark constants for `L` and `C`
— `0.72 0.12` dark, `0.55 0.09` light: the one non-token colour gate 3 in [22-FRONTEND](22-FRONTEND.md)
§7 admits, and only on the five things in the left column. A retired row is achromatic. The glyph library is the
theme's — 122 glyphs in the predecessor — and thirteen of them are refused *as marks* because they are
controls.

**A row may wear a picture** where its list names one, `identity: { picture: <file field> }` (the owner's word of 4 and
9 November: a project changes its logo, a person sets their photo; an organisation keeps its logo too). The picture is
drawn in place of the glyph or the letter wherever the row is: round for a person, in the well's own shape for a thing,
on the slot's tint while it loads, and the glyph or letter again where it cannot be fetched. A payment draws the logo of
the organisation it names beside its payee, as Cash Fish does. Hovering a mark or a face where the reader may write says
Change, and a click opens the changer: a picture chosen, dropped or pasted, cut square and made small before it is sent;
a glyph, for a list that keeps a mark; or none. An agent that finds a logo or a photo sets it where there is none, and
asks before replacing one already chosen.

### 2.2 · Two failures found by measuring a real palette

The starting point for this system was an existing, carefully-built dark/light token set. Computing
the WCAG 2.1 contrast of every ink-on-surface pair from its shipping hex values found two defects
that no amount of review had caught, because contrast is arithmetic and review is not.

**Dark theme, ink on surface** (AA body text needs 4.5:1):

| | sunk | bg | s1 | s2 | s3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ink` | 17.33 | 16.72 | 15.37 | 13.97 | 12.14 |
| `ink2` | 12.40 | 11.97 | 11.00 | 9.99 | 8.69 |
| `dim` | 7.47 | 7.21 | 6.63 | 6.02 | 5.23 |
| `faint` | 5.16 | 4.97 | 4.57 | **4.16** | **3.61** |
| `accent` | 5.24 | 5.06 | 4.65 | **4.22** | **3.67** |

**Light theme:**

| | sunk | bg | s1 | s2 | s3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `dim` | 4.67 | 5.01 | 5.43 | 5.63 | 5.71 |
| `faint` | **3.25** | **3.49** | **3.78** | **3.92** | **3.97** |

1. **The fourth ink level fails AA.** In light it fails on **every** surface. The light ink ramp has
   room for three text levels at AA, not four — so the fix is a widened ramp, not a nudge.
2. **The link colour fails as text on the upper surfaces** in dark (4.22, 3.67) — links inside a
   sheet or a raised card fail while the same link on the page passes.

**And a third, structural:** not one boundary role reaches WCAG 1.4.11's 3:1 for non-text
contrast. Best case **2.36 dark / 2.06 light**. They are legal as decorative hairlines and illegal
as anything load-bearing — a field border, a focus indicator, a control edge. Hence `edge` as a
separate role, ≥3:1 on every surface, used wherever a boundary carries meaning.

> **The lesson generalises past this palette:** an ink ramp is designed by eye and validated by
> arithmetic, and the two disagree at the quiet end. The bottom of the ramp is exactly where a
> designer stops checking and exactly where AA stops being met.

### 2.2b · The corrected values, so the shipped theme passes its own gate

An earlier draft measured these failures, specified a gate that would refuse them, and **did not
record a corrected palette** — which left the house theme illegal on day one. The floor values:

| Role | Dark | Light | Worst ratio after |
|---|---|---|---|
| `faint` | `#8E9C96` | `#5F6C66` | 4.52 dark on `s3` · 4.54 light on `sunk` |
| `dim` | unchanged | `#4C5A54` | 6.1 light on `sunk` |
| `accent` **as text** | `#7A90F0` | unchanged | 4.69 dark on `s3` |
| **`edge`** *(new role)* | `#74837D` | `#74827C` | 3.29 on every surface |

**The light ink ramp has room for three text levels at AA, not four.** So `dim` moves too — the fix
is a widened ramp, not a nudge on one value. These are floors: a theme may go further from the
ground, never closer.

### 2.3 · The finding that changes what a theme gate must check

Intent colours were simulated through dichromat transforms (Viénot single-plane, LMS) and the
contrast measured **between the simulated pairs**. A ratio near 1.00 means two intents render as
the same colour.

> **The finding below is correct and it is the most important measurement in this document. The
> metric is wrong, and [28-THEMING](28-THEMING.md) §4.3 replaces it.** Under simulated dichromacy
> colours collapse onto a *two-dimensional* surface — luminance plus one surviving chromatic axis —
> and contrast ratio measures only luminance. So it produces **false failures** (two intents that
> differ strongly along the surviving axis but share luminance score 1.00 and get refused) and
> **false passes** (two colours thirty luminance points apart clear the floor while being "the same
> colour, lighter"). The gate uses **ΔE2000 on the simulated pair**, which measures the whole
> distance. The numbers below stand as the evidence that produced the rule.

**Dark, deuteranope:**

| Pair | Ratio |
|---|---|
| `earn` vs `danger` | **1.07** |
| `danger` vs `good` | 1.21 |
| `earn` vs `warn` | 1.23 |

**Light, protanope — all ten pairs collapse:**

| Pair | Ratio |
|---|---|
| `earn` vs `accent` | **1.02** |
| `warn` vs `good` | **1.03** |
| `earn` vs `warn` | **1.07** |
| …every remaining pair | ≤ 1.24 |

Green and red are the same olive. For roughly 8% of men, the record's four intent words are one
colour.

**The mechanism is inescapable, and that is the important part.** Tuning five hues to the *same*
contrast target against the *same* ground necessarily gives them the *same luminance*. Remove hue
and nothing is left to tell them apart. Any palette built the obvious, careful, correct-looking way
has this property.

Two consequences:

1. **Colour never carries meaning alone.** The **glyph is part of the intent token**, not a choice
   at the call site. There is exactly one table mapping intent → (role, glyph), and it is the only
   path by which an intent reaches the DOM. A component cannot render `warn` without its glyph
   because there is no API that lets it.

   **Here is that table. An earlier draft promised it and never wrote it — and in its absence the
   tone vocabulary was enumerated five different ways across six documents** (`earn`/`good-value`,
   `good-state`, `good`, `bad`, `danger`, `info`, with and without each). **This is now the only
   list, and it is closed at four:**

   | Intent | Colour role | Glyph | Means |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | `good` | `earn` | ✓ | resting, healthy, done |
   | `warn` | `warn` | ⚠ | watch this |
   | `bad` | `danger` | ⊘ | wrong, overdue, failing |
   | `info` | `accent` | ⓘ | a note, no judgement |

   **Four words an agent may write: `good` · `warn` · `bad` · `info`.** `earn` and `danger` are
   *colour roles* a theme maps — never words an author types. In a declaration an unknown tone is
   `ENUM_VALUE_UNKNOWN` at install; in the dialect the tone is a trailing word on a block's heading,
   and a word that is neither a tone nor an alias is a cell, never a refusal
   ([05-BLOCKS](05-BLOCKS-AND-THEMES.md) §2.1, [34-GRAMMARS](34-GRAMMARS.md) §2.2). **The four
   aliases in [05-BLOCKS](05-BLOCKS-AND-THEMES.md) §7.4 (`earn→good` `danger→bad` `accent→info`
   `risk→warn`) resolve to the intent, and `hole` is a `bar`-only tone** — which is what makes the
   ten-pair collapse measured above survivable: four intents, four distinct glyphs. The renderer
   emits the four tone glyphs itself with `role="img"` and an `aria-label`; a `chips` glyph is a
   `data-glyph` cell the host stylesheet draws ([36-SPEC-RENDER](36-SPEC-RENDER.md) §5).
2. **The theme gate must check intent separability, not only text contrast.** A validator that
   checks every text-on-surface pair would certify this palette — and certify precisely the failure
   it exists to prevent.

### 2.4 · The theme gate — eight checks here, sixteen in total

Run at install, in CI, and live in the theme editor. A failing theme is **refused**, naming the
pair.

| # | Check | Floor |
|---|---|---|
| A | every ink × every surface | 4.5:1 |
| B | `accent` as text × every surface | 4.5:1 |
| C | `faint` × every surface at the large-text floor — the palette half; the usage half, no `faint` on text below 24 px (`t-xl`), is a `css-rules.mjs` rule in the web app ([22-FRONTEND](22-FRONTEND.md) §7) | 3:1 |
| D | `edge` and the three `-fill` roles × every surface | 3:1 |
| E | focus ring × both its neighbours, and focused × unfocused; the two ring tones 9:1 apart | 3:1 (2.4.13) |
| F | **intent × intent, ΔE2000 after Brettel-1997 simulation, all three deficiencies at severity 1.0** | **≥20, or ≥10 with a distinct glyph** |
| G | intent as text × every surface | 4.5:1 |
| H | the surface ladder is monotonic in **relative luminance**, in both modes | — |

One dependency-free file. It is the single piece of code that makes "themes are safe" true rather
than claimed.

> **Eight is the floor, not the total.** [28-THEMING](28-THEMING.md) §4.4 adds eight more — the
> elevation ladder, sRGB gamut, hue-only encoding, forced-colors survival, `line` vs `edge`, an
> APCA advisory, a re-serialising parse, and the banned-value list. Two of those, **I** and **L**,
> catch failures nothing here notices: a theme is exactly the artefact that breaks the elevation
> ladder [05-BLOCKS](05-BLOCKS-AND-THEMES.md) §6 promises, and forced colours silently deletes the
> surface ladder §2.1 rests on. Also corrected there: **Brettel 1997, not Viénot 1999** — the
> single-plane simplification is valid for protan and deutan only, and its reference implementation
> carries a literal warning that it is not accurate for tritanopia. And four of the sixteen — C, D
> and E above, and K there (`accent-text` at least 8 L* from `ink`) — are written as palette
> arithmetic the generator passes by construction ([39-SPEC-THEME](39-SPEC-THEME.md) §4).

## 3 · Typography

### 3.1 · The families

| Role | Family | Why |
|---|---|---|
| UI and prose | a humanist grotesque with a large x-height and true tabular figures | one family for labels and paragraphs keeps the page coherent; the x-height carries 11px |
| Identifiers and code | the system monospace stack — no webfont ([22-FRONTEND](22-FRONTEND.md) §9); the *role*, not a chosen face, is what makes `#125` and `D41` read as identifiers | `0/O` and `1/l/I` ambiguity is accepted as the cost of zero bytes |
| Arabic | **IBM Plex Sans Arabic** | open licence, a real companion design rather than a fallback, and it is engineered alongside a Latin sibling |

Latin and Arabic are joined by `unicode-range` on the `@font-face`, so an all-Latin page never
downloads the Arabic subset.

### 3.2 · Numerals — the rule that is counter-intuitive

> **Mono for identity. Tabular-proportional for quantity.**

A monospace face gives the thousands separator a full cell, so `$2,869` renders as `$2 , 869` at
display size — the comma floats in the middle of the number and the figure reads as wider and
smaller than the sans figure beside it. That is wrong for money.

So: `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` on a proportional face for every **quantity**, and the
monospace face only for **identifiers** — ids, hashes, tokens, code. Two separate token roles, and
a lint rule that the identifier role may not co-occur with a display size.

### 3.3 · Arabic sits at a different optical size

Arabic has no capitals and no x-height; its letterforms carry more of their weight below and above
the baseline, and diacritics need room. Matched by point size to Latin, it reads smaller and
cramped.

| Adjustment | Value | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Font size multiplier | ~1.12× | **low — a practitioner range, not a measurement** |
| Line height addition | ~+0.18em | **low, same** |
| `letter-spacing` | **exactly 0. Always.** | certain — Arabic is a joined script; tracking breaks the joins |

The first two are marked low-confidence deliberately, and
[17-OPEN-QUESTIONS](17-OPEN-QUESTIONS.md) carries the 20-line measurement that settles them: render
ب/ه/م against the Latin x-height and read the boxes. Guessing is acceptable; *not saying it is a
guess* is not.

## 4 · Density and the measure

### 4.1 · Width is a property of the block kind, not of the page

The rule that resolves prose-versus-tables, and it is the third time this family of bug has been
paid for:

| Class | Applies to | Width |
|---|---|---|
| `measure` | paragraphs, lists, blockquotes, headings, `note` | ~72 characters |
| `room` | `table`, `spec`, `params`, `comparison`, `bar`, `gauge` | the full column |
| `content` | `stat`, `chips`, and any short drawn block | **its own content, not its column** |

The `content` class exists because of a measured failure: a three-cell grid using CSS `auto-fit`
generated **seven tracks** for three items in a 962 px column — four of them empty, a 550 px slab
of border colour. Every selector was correct.

> **A layout primitive must be told how many things it contains. It must never infer the count from
> the space available.**

And `note` is the only drawn kind in the `measure` class, because it is the only one that may
contain a paragraph. Prose in any other block is a **parse-time error node at render**, not a style:
the block degrades to its visible fence (05 §3) with the last sentence of §6's table above it. The
write is **accepted** — a `document` is never refused for its dialect, because the visible fence is
the feedback channel ([34-GRAMMARS](34-GRAMMARS.md) §2.2 has the row shape per kind). An agent told
to "summarise in a stat block" will otherwise put a sentence in a cell, and the block will be
unreadable in a way the agent cannot see.

### 4.2 · The table, computed rather than chosen

| Property | Value | Derivation |
|---|---|---|
| Column ceiling | **10** | at 1440px the return sweep is ~1392px; beyond ten columns of legible width the eye loses the row, and in a money ledger that means reading a figure off the wrong line |
| Row height | **28 / 32 / 36 px** for `compact` / `comfortable` / `roomy` — the reader's Density setting, labelled Compact / Comfortable / Roomy; a theme sets the enum, never a pixel | never the target: Targets are designed at 44 × 44 CSS px (SC 2.5.5 AAA, by choice — SC 2.5.8 AA's 24 × 24 is the legal floor) through an invisible hit box; CI measures every control under a coarse pointer, counting `::before`/`::after`, and fails below 43.5 × 43.5 on either axis. |
| Alignment | quantities, dates and counts **end**; text and refs **start** | a decimal point that moves is a column you cannot scan |
| Truncation | text truncates at the end; **identifiers truncate in the middle**, keeping prefix and suffix; **figures never truncate** | a truncated figure is not shortened, it is *wrong* |
| Row anchor | a persistent focus band, not hover | hover does not help a keyboard user and does not survive a glance away |
| Sticky | header row, and the first column | the two things you look back to |

Recovery from truncation is opening the row, not a `title=` tooltip. Information required to act may
never live only in a tooltip.

### 4.3 · No virtualisation — a cap, and keyset pages

Rows arrive in **200-row keyset pages** into rowgroups of 100 under `content-visibility`; the DOM caps
at **2,000** with a visible cap line, and beyond it the view is a filter or an export — never a
virtualised scroll ([22-FRONTEND](22-FRONTEND.md) §4; [50-SPEC-WEB](50-SPEC-WEB.md) §5.2).
Virtualisation breaks find-in-page, breaks `aria-rowcount` unless carefully maintained, and breaks
the scrollbar as a position cue; and the measured wall is DOM construction, which virtualising
would only hide. INP on the target phone with 500 rows painted is a release checklist row until an
ADB harness exists ([18-BUILD-TOOLKIT](18-BUILD-TOOLKIT.md) §6).

## 5 · Empty states, and the cold start

The record is empty on day one, and that is the likeliest recorded cause of death. So an empty
state is a **class of designed thing**, not an afterthought.

### 5.1 · The first sixty seconds

The first screen is **not a dashboard**. A dashboard of `—` and flat charts teaches, in one glance,
that the app is empty and none of it is for you. So a record with no lists of its own never draws
Home: the root layout sends it to `/start` (`apps/web/src/routes/+layout.ts`), and the kernel's own
`session` list is not counted as one.

What it meets there is one question and one press. "Who are you?", answered in a sentence with four
chips to start it; then a preview of the BrainDog that sentence makes, its pages and its Home drawn
with examples that say none of them is saved, and nothing written; then **Make it mine**, which
installs it, writes the person's own People row and the entries the sentence filled, and lands on
Home. It needs no model, and it refuses no sentence.

> **The first run is specified in exactly one place: [19-INTERFACE](19-INTERFACE.md) §6.** The
> paragraph above is its summary. Two earlier designs are gone: an outline plus an autofocused field
> plus pack selection on one screen, which put a text field and a checkbox list in the same tab
> order, and a row typed by hand into one chosen list, with pack selection on a second screen.

A record with lists and nothing pinned, waiting or due is not a first run, and Home says so calmly:
"Nothing needs you right now.", and one line on what will fill the page as the lists fill
([19-INTERFACE](19-INTERFACE.md) §2).

**Instrumented targets, held as hypotheses:** first non-empty screen inside 60 s, ten facts in the
record by 5 minutes. If the median first write is over 90 s, the first screen is wrong.

### 5.2 · Every empty state names its cause

**Five causes, five different messages** — the canonical list is [19-INTERFACE](19-INTERFACE.md) §6, which names them `no-data-yet`, `no-results`, `cleared`, `no-permission` and `error`. This section adds the *content* of each; it does not define the set. Collapsing them into one "Nothing here yet" is the failure.

| State | Says | Offers |
|---|---|---|
| `no-data-yet` | what this register is *for*, in one line — and *"An agent with your token can import them."* | the add field, focused; **Ask Slash** only when a model is configured; the exact MCP prompt, copyable |
| `no-results` | *"38 rows, none matching `overdue`"* — the query quoted verbatim | clear the filter |
| `cleared` | *"Nothing outstanding. The last one was paid 6 days ago."* | nothing — this is good news |
| `no-permission` | who holds access to this register | Request access |
| `error` | what failed, once, in the house voice | Try again, and a copyable request id |

The agent prompt inside `no-data-yet` is specific to this product and is the highest-value line: the
user has agents, and the empty state is the right place to say so. An earlier draft had four rows
here — none for `no-permission` or `error`, and a fourth, *"an agent could fill this"*, that is not a
state but the prompt line of the first.

## 6 · Refusals

This system refuses a lot — a theme failing contrast, a metric with no target, a pack conflict, a
mixed-currency sum, a `starts` pattern over 40 bytes. **The refusal is the main teaching surface**,
so its copy is designed.

**The shape: what was wrong · where · what to do.** No apology, no blame, no exclamation. Every
refusal sentence the app shows is the server's `fix`, verbatim — never rewritten client-side; the
app's own copy lives in one file, `copy.ts` ([50-SPEC-WEB](50-SPEC-WEB.md) §3).

| Refusal |
|---|
| `theme.midnight — the pair (faint on s3) is 3.61:1. Body text needs 4.5:1. Lighten faint to at least #8E9C96, or use it only above 24px.` |
| `theme.midnight — good and bad are ΔE 6.2 apart to a deuteranope. Four intents need at least 10. Move one hue, or lower the chroma of the other.` |
| `metric.unpaid — a number with no target is decoration, and a number with no comparison window cannot be acted on. Add target: and window:.` |
| `metric.total — sums obligations.amount across 3 currencies (EGP, USD, GBP). Name an fx source and date, or scope the predicate to one currency.` |
| `register.wide — 71 fields. The ceiling is 64. A register with more than that is a database, not a register.` |
| `rule.overdue.when — the 'starts' pattern is 63 bytes. The cap is 40. Use 'matches', which searches the full-text index instead.` |
| `freelancer-uk and money-core both provide register.invoices. Uninstall one, or ask the author to rename.` |
| `register.obligations → register.bills renames a spoken id. #125 in a note would stop resolving. Change the display name instead; it is free.` |
| `block.invoice-card sets 'colour: #E11D48'. A block names intent — warn, bad, good, info — and the theme names appearance.` |
| `The note is inside a stat cell. A stat cell is a figure; a paragraph in one is unreadable. Move the prose between the blocks.` |

A refusal is never a toast. A toast for a blocking error disappears before it has been read.

The last row is the exception that proves the shape: it is **not a write refusal**. It is the line
drawn above a degraded block at render — the dialect's two codes, `DIALECT_ARITY` and
`DIALECT_KIND_UNKNOWN`, are render-time degradations, never refusals ([30-BLUEPRINT](30-BLUEPRINT.md)
§6). The other nine refuse an install or a write.

### And the malformed-block rule

> A malformed block **degrades to a visible fence** showing exactly what the author typed, with the
> reason above it.

The parser has **no path that returns nothing**. Every failure returns an error block. Silent
omission in a record whose value is completeness is data loss with a tidy appearance — and when the
author is an agent, the visible fence is the only feedback channel that exists.

## 7 · Accessibility

WCAG 2.2 AA is the floor, and these are the parts that need a decision rather than a checkbox.

| Area | Decision |
|---|---|
| Focus | a **two-tone** ring — an inner and an outer colour — so it is visible on every surface in both themes. `outline: none` is a CI failure — the row is in [18-BUILD-TOOLKIT](18-BUILD-TOOLKIT.md) §6 |
| Focus not obscured (2.4.11) | `scroll-padding-block` set to the sticky header plus the tab bar plus the safe area. Otherwise keyboard focus lands under the header |
| The table | **`role="grid"`, with the full APG grid keyboard contract implemented** — a roving `tabindex` on the row, arrows, `Home`/`End`, `⌘Home`/`⌘End`, `PageUp`/`PageDown`, `aria-sort`, and `aria-rowcount`/`aria-rowindex` when rows are windowed. It is wrapped in a labelled `role="region"` with `tabindex="0"` so a keyboard user can scroll it. **This is not optional and an earlier draft wrongly hedged it:** [19-INTERFACE](19-INTERFACE.md) §4 makes the grid load-bearing — it is what flips a screen reader out of browse mode so bare single-letter shortcuts are legal under WCAG 2.1.4, and it is the difference between one tab stop and four thousand. A half-implemented grid is indeed worse than a good table, so the contract is a CI-asserted checklist rather than an aspiration |
| Drawn figures | a `stat` cell is a label, a value and a delta — three text nodes with an accessible name that reads as a sentence, not three floating numbers. `role="meter"` is avoided: support is inconsistent enough to be a risk |
| Targets | Targets are designed at 44 × 44 CSS px (SC 2.5.5 AAA, by choice — SC 2.5.8 AA's 24 × 24 is the legal floor) through an invisible hit box; CI measures every control under a coarse pointer, counting `::before`/`::after`, and fails below 43.5 × 43.5 on either axis. That one sentence is the rule everywhere the number appears — an earlier draft of this row wrongly cited 2.5.8 for 44×44, and other documents said 44, 43.5 and 24 in six places. Spacing counts under 2.5.8. **Both axes** — a control grown only in height measures 38×44 and fails. **The hit box includes `::before` and `::after`** because a chevron drawn as a 20 px glyph inside a 44 px box is a 20 px target if the pseudo-element is what takes the pointer |
| **Rows** | every child of a row shares a vertical centre within **1.5 px**, or the row wraps. This is the measurement behind *"controls do not sit on rows being read"* — a control that sits 3 px low reads as belonging to the row below |
| **Contrast target, not floor** | the gate is AA. The house theme targets **≥ 5.4:1 for `ink` and `ink2`**, because the predecessor measured 5.4 on a phone in daylight at 14.5 px and found AA's 4.5 was what stopped being readable. Its rule is blunt: *"Contrast is measured and fine. Do not 'fix' it."* |
| **The type scale is a token, and raising it is not a fix** | a fixed eight steps, 12 → 34 px: `t-xs` 12 · `t-sm` 13 · `t-base` 15 · `t-md` 17 · `t-lg` 20 · `t-xl` 24 · `t-2xl` 28 · `t-3xl` 34. The reader's **Text** setting picks the body step among `t-sm` / `t-base` / `t-md` (Small / Normal / Large); **a theme never sets type** ([28-THEMING](28-THEMING.md) §1). The predecessor raised it twice on the theory that *hard to read* meant *too small*, and both were rejected: *"if a screen is hard to read, the fault is in what it says and how it is arranged. Use weight, colour and space."* |
| Reduced motion | a *promise*, not a preference. Durations collapse to ~1 ms and the state simply changes |
| Reduced transparency | honoured where the query exists — Safari has never shipped `prefers-reduced-transparency` ([28-THEMING](28-THEMING.md) §2). Since depth is a luminance ladder rather than blur, there is nothing to remove on the browsers that lack it |
| Authentication (3.3.8) | passkeys require no cognitive test. Paste is never blocked on the fallback code field |
| The command palette | `<dialog>`, the combobox pattern, and a **debounced** `role="status"` for the result count — an undebounced live region announces on every keystroke and is unusable |

## 8 · Internationalisation and RTL

Arabic is a real requirement, not a hypothetical. The plan is therefore complete rather than
gestural.

| Concern | Rule |
|---|---|
| Layout | **logical properties only** — `margin-inline-start`, never `margin-left`. A CI grep on physical properties fails the build |
| Bidirectional text | `<bdi>` around every interpolated value, applied **once in the renderer**. Without it a Latin id inside an Arabic sentence reorders around adjacent punctuation |
| Numerals | **Latin digits pinned for every figure** (`-u-nu-latn`), because Eastern Arabic digits break tabular alignment. Prose may use the locale's digits; a ledger may not |
| Dates and currency | `Intl.NumberFormat` / `Intl.DateTimeFormat` with an explicit locale. Never a hand-rolled symbol map |
| Mirrors | layout, nav, icons with a direction (back/forward), progress fills |
| **Must NOT mirror** | **time axes, timelines, and anything where left-to-right means earlier-to-later.** Mirroring a revenue chart inverts whether it is going up |
| Verification | a screenshot matrix with `dir` as an axis, plus a pseudo-locale |

The time-axis decision is marked **medium confidence** and the test is written down: show one plot
mirrored and unmirrored to three Arabic-first readers who work with money and ask which is going up.
Hesitation on the unmirrored one means mirror it.

## 9 · Motion

**Open and close are not symmetric.** A panel opens in `--d-enter` (280 ms) and closes in `--d-exit`
(160 ms); a chip changes state in `--d-state` (180 ms). *Opening is something you watch; closing is
something you have already decided about.* An earlier draft opened this section with *"400 ms and
350"*, which is above its own ceiling below and 120 ms off the token table for the same element. And a
state class is toggled **only when the state changes** — toggling one that carries a
transform on every `touchmove` restarts the animation sixty times a second and it never plays.

The predecessor's token values, carried as the starting point rather than re-derived:

| Token | ms | For |
|---|---|---|
| `--d-press` / `--d-release` | 90 / 180 | a control answering a held press, and letting go |
| `--d-state` | 180 | a tick, a chip, a segment thumb |
| `--d-move` | 240 | a row relocating, a screen arriving |
| `--d-enter` / `--d-exit` | 280 / 160 | a sheet, a menu, a panel — **open is longer than close, always** |

The spring is a `linear()` easing with ζ = 0.7467 and ω = 6.56 — **it ends at exactly 1 and peaks at
1.05 or below**, asserted by a test, because a spring that overshoots and settles at 0.998 leaves a
one-pixel gap on every panel forever. Under reduced motion, `--d-move` is 1 ms, scale is 1, the curve
is linear, and **every state still changes something** — *replace, not delete*.


A budget, because the failure mode is specific and fatal for this buyer: **an app that feels like it
is running underwater.** A technical user goes back to the CLI.

| Class | Duration | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| State — hover, press, release, tone change | 90–180 ms | the most frequent transition; the budget is set by frequency |
| Move — a row travelling, a screen arriving | 180–260 ms | spatial continuity |
| Overlay — a sheet, a dialog, a panel | 220–300 ms in, `--d-exit` (160 ms) out — exit faster than enter | arrival and departure |
| **Ceiling** | **300 ms** | nothing is longer |

Four rules:

1. **No chaining.** Three chained 280 ms transitions is 840 ms of not being able to act.
2. **Input is never blocked** by an animation. Every transition is interruptible.
3. **Never animate a focus ring.** Keyboard navigation becomes wading. Also on the do-not-animate
   list: table sort, filter results, and anything that fires per keystroke.
4. **Never `* { transition: background-color, color }`.** It works once and then every hover, focus,
   selection and press in the application is fading forever. A theme change is a snapshot cross-fade
   — a discrete event — not a rule.

Budget: **INP under 100 ms** to first paint of a response, measured on the target phone, failing CI
([18-BUILD-TOOLKIT](18-BUILD-TOOLKIT.md) §6).

## 10 · The phone

Not a shrunken desktop. Two breakpoints and one rule: the sidebar becomes the tab bar at 900 px,
rows become cards by default below 640 px, and target size follows the pointer, never the width
([19-INTERFACE](19-INTERFACE.md) §3). At 390 px:

| Surface | On a phone |
|---|---|
| Register list | rows become two-line cards showing the register's three declared `mobile:` fields ([02-DATA-MODEL](02-DATA-MODEL.md) §5) — a declaration, not a media query. Not a scrolling table |
| A 500-row table | the scroll wrapper survives, but the default view is the card list; the table is an explicit choice |
| Nav | the tab bar carries the spine — Home · Decide · Inbox · More — and a sheet carries the declared nav ([19-INTERFACE](19-INTERFACE.md) §3); the palette is a header button, not a fifth tab |
| The palette | full-screen, keyboard-first, because typing is how you navigate on a phone too |
| Authoring | **read and approve, do not author.** The page builder and pack builder are desktop surfaces. Approving a decision or a proposal is a phone surface — and it is the one that matters, because approval is time-sensitive |
| Safe areas | insets on every fixed element, paid exactly once — paying twice is a real, easy bug |
