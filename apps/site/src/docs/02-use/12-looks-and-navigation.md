# Looks and navigation

How BrainDog looks and where its navigation sits are yours to choose. Some choices are for you on this device, some follow you to every device, and one, the shared look, is for everybody who opens your BrainDog.

## Whose choice is it

```comparison | Where each choice is kept
Choice | Who it is for | Where
Accent, scheme, contrast, text size, density | You, on this device | Settings, under Appearance
The shared look | Everybody who opens your BrainDog | Settings, under Appearance
Navigation and the phone bar | You, on every device you sign in on | Settings, under Appearance
Order and hidden pages | You, on every device you sign in on | Settings, under Navigation
```

## Appearance

- **Accent**: the colour BrainDog wears for you, from a row of swatches, or House for the house colour. A colour that would make text unreadable, or would make good and wrong look alike to somebody who does not see red and green apart, is refused with the reason.
- **Colour scheme**: Light, System or Dark. System follows your device, and you can always go back to it.
- **More contrast**: makes text and edges stand out more, and keeps your accent.
- **Text**: Small, Normal or Large.
- **Density**: Compact, Comfortable or Roomy, with a preview of rows at each.

## How your BrainDog looks

The shared look, under **How your BrainDog looks**, is every colour your BrainDog is drawn in, in light and in dark, for everybody who opens it. An accent you picked for your device still wins on your device.

```steps
Press Edit the look | Each colour is listed by what it is for: the page, the panel, text, money in, needs you | Settings, under Appearance
Change a colour | Nudge it darker, lighter, duller, richer, warmer or cooler, pick it from a grid, or enter a colour code | In the editor
Read the checks | Every pair of colours that meet is marked Fails, Tight or Fine, in light and in dark | Under the colours
Fix what fails | Each failing pair offers a fix, such as Darken quiet text | Under the colours
Save the look | The owner saves it. A colleague sends it to the owner in Needs you | At the foot of the editor
```

The editor also sets the corners: Square, Soft or Round. **Use the house look** puts it back in the house look at any time.

Nothing that fails can be saved: the look waits until every pair marked Fails is fixed. A plugin that brings a look with colours that cannot be read offers **Fix and install**, with a fix ready for each colour, for you to check before it goes in.

## The design book

**Design book**, beside the look in Settings, shows the language BrainDog's own pages are written in: every kind of block with an example, the colours, type and spacing. It is read only and drawn from the same source the look uses. An agent reads the same book at `braindog:design` before it designs a page.

## Navigation

Choose how your navigation sits on a laptop, in Settings under Appearance:

```comparison | Navigation on a laptop
Choice | What it does
Top bar | The sidebar sits beside your work on a wide window, and narrows to a strip of icons on a smaller one
One button | Keeps nothing on screen. One button opens the whole sidebar over the page, and a search button sits in the corner
```

The sidebar holds every place: Today, Needs you, Work, Money, Agents and People, then your projects. The top bar says where you are and lets you ask, with the bell and your face beside it. Press [ to shrink the sidebar to its icons and press it again to bring it back.

On a phone, BrainDog uses a tab bar whichever you choose: Today and up to three more places, then Menu, which holds the rest. Pick the three under **Phone bar**, in Settings under Appearance.

BrainDog may offer a page for a list that keeps growing, but it never places one in your navigation for you. See [Today and pages](/docs/use/home-and-pages).

## Arrange the sidebar

- **Move up** and **Move down** on an entry's menu change its place in its section.
- **Hide** takes a page out of your navigation without deleting it. A hidden page stays in BrainDog, in search and in the palette. Settings, under Navigation, lists what is hidden, and Show puts it back in the place it had.
- g then a digit from 1 to 9 opens that entry, in your order.

Your order, your hidden pages and your navigation choices are kept with you in your BrainDog, so they follow you to another device.

## Keyboard

Single letters act on whatever is in front of you: / to find, g to go somewhere, ? for the key sheet, j and k to move. **Character keys**, in Settings under Keyboard, turns every single-letter shortcut off. Shortcuts that use Ctrl or Alt keep working, so Ctrl+J (⌘J on a Mac) still opens Slash to ask.
