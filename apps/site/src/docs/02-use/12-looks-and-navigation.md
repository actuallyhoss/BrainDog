# Looks and navigation

How BrainDog looks and where its navigation sits are yours to choose. Some choices are for you on this device, some follow you to every device, and one, the record's look, is for everybody who opens it.

## Whose choice is it

```comparison | Where each choice is kept
Choice | Who it is for | Where
Accent, scheme, contrast, text size, density | You, on this device | Settings, under Appearance
The record's look | Everybody who opens the record | Settings, under Appearance
Navigation shape, order, hidden pages | You, on every device you sign in on | Settings, under Navigation
```

## Appearance

- **Accent**: the colour the record wears for you, from a row of swatches, or House for the house colour. A colour that would make text unreadable, or would make good and wrong look alike to somebody who does not see red and green apart, is refused with the reason.
- **Colour scheme**: Light, System or Dark. System follows your device, and you can always go back to it.
- **More contrast**: makes text and edges stand out more, and keeps your accent.
- **Text**: Small, Normal or Large.
- **Density**: Compact, Comfortable or Roomy, with a preview of rows at each.

## The record's look

The record's look is every colour it is drawn in, in light and in dark, for everybody who opens it. An accent you picked for your device still wins on your device.

```steps
Press Edit the look | Each colour is listed by what it is for: the page, the panel, text, money in, needs you | Settings, under Appearance
Change a colour | Nudge it darker, lighter, duller, richer, warmer or cooler, pick it from a grid, or enter a colour code | In the editor
Read the checks | Every pair of colours that meet is marked Fails, Tight or Fine, in light and in dark | Under the colours
Fix what fails | Each failing pair offers a fix, such as Darken quiet text | Under the colours
Save the look | The owner saves it. A colleague sends it to the owner in Needs you | At the foot of the editor
```

The editor also sets the corners: Square, Soft or Round. **Use the house look** puts the record back in the house look at any time.

Nothing that fails can be saved: the look waits until every pair marked Fails is fixed. An add-on that brings a look with colours that cannot be read offers **Fix and install**, with a fix ready for each colour, for you to check before it goes in.

## Navigation shapes

Choose where your navigation lives, in Settings under Navigation:

```comparison | The three shapes
Shape | What it does
Sidebar | Holds a list of any length, in sections you arrange
Top bar | Holds a few entries in one strip, and puts the rest behind More, saying how many are there
Menu | Keeps nothing on screen, and opens the whole list when you ask for it
```

On a phone, BrainDog uses a tab bar whatever the shape, because a narrow screen has room for nothing else. Ctrl and the backslash key (⌘ and backslash on a Mac) collapse the sidebar and bring it back.

## A shape recommended for you

BrainDog's design agent reads what your record holds and how you use it, and may recommend a shape that suits it better, with one sentence saying why. It appears in Settings under Navigation as **A shape that might suit this record better**, with **Switch to** that shape. It never switches for you, and once you dismiss a recommendation it does not come back.

The same rule holds for pages: the record may offer a page for a list that keeps growing, but it never places one in your navigation for you. See [Home and pages](/docs/use/home-and-pages).

## Arrange the sidebar

- **Move up**, **Move down** and **Move to top** on an entry's menu change its place in its section.
- **Hide** takes a page out of your navigation without deleting it. A hidden page stays in the record, in search and in the palette. Settings, under Navigation, lists what is hidden, and Show puts it back in the place it had.
- g then a digit from 1 to 9 opens that entry, in your order.

Your order, your hidden pages and your shape are kept with you on the record, so they follow you to another device.

## Keyboard

Single letters act on whatever is in front of you: / for the palette, g to go somewhere, ? for the key sheet, j and k to move. **Character keys**, in Settings under Keyboard, turns every single-letter shortcut off. Shortcuts that use Ctrl or Alt keep working.
