# Home and pages

Home is the page your record opens on. It answers four questions without a filter: what needs you, who is working, what is coming, and what happened while you were not looking. Every other page is built the same way, out of blocks, and you can arrange any of them.

## What Home shows

Home is drawn from your use case's own Home where it has one, and from the house Home otherwise. Either way it holds the same kinds of thing:

- **What needs you**: questions waiting on Decide, bills falling due, anything a rule raised. Answer a question or tick a row where it is drawn, without leaving Home.
- **Right now**: the few figures that matter most, such as cash now or open work.
- **Since you were away**: what you, your colleagues, your agents and your rules changed since you last looked. Home counts "since" from when you last left it, not from when you opened it, so nothing slips past while you are reading.

Home keeps itself up to date. When an agent, a rule or another tab changes something, Home reads the change without a reload.

A colleague who holds some of your lists and not the whole record sees a Home that names their lists and opens them in one press.

## Pages

A page gathers what you keep an eye on: figures, charts, tables of rows, boards, calendars, notes. Each block reads from your lists, so a page is never out of date and never holds rows of its own. Removing a page removes nothing from your lists.

Pages sit in the sidebar in sections. Some pages are drawn once for each row, such as a page for each project, from one shared layout.

## Arrange a page

Press **Edit layout** at the top of a page.

```steps
Move a block | Drag it, or use Move up and Move down. On a wide page, move it between the main column and the side rail | In Edit layout
Change its width | Pick a width from its menu, up to the full width of the page | In Edit layout
Add a block | Press Add a block, say what you want to show, then choose how to draw it | In Edit layout
Remove a block | Remove this block takes it off the page. The rows it read stay in their list | In Edit layout
Keep it | Press Done. Until then, nothing is saved | At the top of the page
```

Adding a block asks two things. **Show me** is what you want: say something, show a figure, show a list, show a plan, or show media. **Draw it as** is the kind, each with a line about what it is for:

```comparison | Some of the blocks you can add
Block | What it is for
Figures | Two or three figures across the top
Bar chart | Amounts side by side, biggest first
Parts of a whole | One total cut into its parts
Board | Cards in columns by state
Table of rows | A list, in full
To-do list | Things to do, with a date
Numbered steps | A sequence, numbered
What happened | What agents, rules and you did since you last looked
Note | A paragraph that matters
```

A page drawn once for each row cannot be rearranged from one of those rows, because every row shares its layout. If a page changes while you arrange it, BrainDog asks you to open it again first.

## Put a list on a page

On any list, filter it down to what you want to watch, then choose **Put this on a page** (Alt+B). It becomes a block on a page, reading the same filter, so the page shows those rows as they change.

## Ask Slash for a page

Press / and describe what you want to see. Slash drafts the page and puts it on Decide, showing what will appear, what each block reads from and where it goes in the sidebar. Nothing is placed until you accept. See [Slash](/docs/use/slash).

## Pages the record suggests

When a list grows, or you keep asking the same question, your record can offer a page for it. It never places one for you. Offers wait in Settings, under Navigation, until you promote one or dismiss it, and **Suggest new pages** there turns the offers off. Pages you have already placed are untouched either way.

## How a page is built

Every page is written in a small text format: Markdown, with fenced blocks for the figures. **How this is built** on a page shows its source, and you can download it as a file. This page of the docs is written in the same format. The [page dialect](/docs/dialect/overview) section describes it.

## Share one page

**Share** on a page makes a read-only link. Anybody with it can read that page as it is now, and nothing else: they cannot change anything or open any other page. You choose how long it works, and you can close it early in Settings, under Links. The link is shown once, so copy it when it appears.

## Moving around

- / opens the palette, to search, jump anywhere, or ask Slash.
- g then h goes Home; g then a digit opens that item of your sidebar.
- On a page, j and k move between blocks, and o opens the list behind one.
- ? shows every key.
