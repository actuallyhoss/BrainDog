# Lists

A list holds one kind of thing you keep track of: bills, clients, tasks, subscriptions, domains. Each thing in it is a row, and each row has the fields its list declares. Lists are in the sidebar, and every page reads from them.

## Add a row

There are two ways, one quick and one full.

```steps
Type its name | The line at the top of the list takes a name. Press Enter and the row is added | On the list
Or open the full form | New (or n) opens every field the list declares, with the ones it needs marked | On the list
Fill in the rest later | Open the row and change any field, or add a note | On the row
```

When the quick line recognises something in what you typed, such as a date or an amount, it fills that field in and says so, and you can change any of it on the row. The full form's button stays off until the fields the list needs are filled in, and a value that cannot be stored as typed says why under its field.

On a phone the quick line sits behind the + at the top, and the list is drawn as cards.

Passwords and keys never go in a row. They go in the Keychain, and a row records what one opens.

## Import your contacts

To bring in the people you already know, open People and choose **Import contacts** from its ⋯ menu, or from search. People with nobody in it but you says the same under its heading.

```steps
Choose the file | A CSV from Google, Outlook, Apple or LinkedIn, or a vCard (.vcf) from your phone | On Import contacts
Check the preview | How many people and organisations it adds, the new organisations by name, and who is already here | Before anything is added
Press Add | New organisations are made first, then each person is linked to theirs | On the same page
Tidy what the import left | One question for all of it: names written all small or all capitals get capitals, a new organisation gets the website its people's emails share, and new organisations can be marked as clients where you keep clients. Yes does all of it, Not now leaves it | Right after
```

Anybody already in People with the same email is skipped, and so is a second copy of the same person inside the file. An organisation you already keep is matched by its name, without Ltd, Inc or Co, and the people are linked to the one you have.

## Find rows

- **Search** reads the fields the box names. For anything else, use the filters.
- **Filters** narrow the list by a field's value, a date before or after a day, and more. The filters in use read as a sentence above the rows, and Clear filters takes them all off.
- **Display** (or v) changes what you see and nothing else: the order, and which columns are shown. It is kept for you, per list.

A list draws up to 2,000 rows at a time and says so when it stops there. The way past is a filter or an export.

## Save a view

Once a list is filtered or searched, **Save as a view** keeps what is on screen under a name. Views are listed under Views in the sidebar, and a view is the same list with the same rows, only fewer of them. Back to the saved view undoes any filter you add on top.

**Put this on a page** does the same for a page: the filter becomes a block. See [Today and pages](/docs/use/home-and-pages).

## Change rows

- **One field**: click into a cell, or press e, and change it in place. The row shows Saving, then Saved.
- **A row's state**: set it from the row's menu, or drag a card across a board.
- **Many rows**: select them (x, or Shift+x to extend), then set their state or archive them at once. BrainDog says how many landed.
- **Undo**: Ctrl+Z (⌘Z on a Mac) undoes your last change, and the toast after a change offers Put back.

If somebody else changed the row first, nothing is written over their change. The row shows the latest version and asks you to check it and try again.

## Archive and put back

Archive takes a row out of every list until it is put back. Press Delete on a row, use Archive in its menu, or select several and choose Archive them. The toast offers Put back at once, and an archived row keeps a Put back of its own when you open it.

Archiving is how rows leave a list in the app. Nothing a person archives is gone: History keeps every change.

## The row itself

Open a row (Enter, or click its title) to see all of it:

- every field, each one changeable in place;
- notes, added with **Add a note**;
- comments, added with **Add a comment**, where writing a colleague's name with @ brings them in;
- what points at this row from other lists, and through which field;
- when it was added, and by whom: you, a colleague, an agent or a rule.

While you have a row open, a change somebody else makes to it shows by itself, and comments arrive without a reload.

Press p on a list to peek at a row without leaving the list, o to open the list a linked field points at, and y to copy a row's number (Shift+Y copies its link).

## History

**History** in the sidebar lists every change in your BrainDog: who made it, what kind of change it was, and when. Filter it by who, by kind or by day. It has no delete button, because a history that can be edited cannot be trusted.

Every change moves BrainDog's version on by one, so a version names a moment.

## Where lists come from

A list comes from your first run, from a plugin in **Plugins**, or from a proposal by Slash or an agent that you accept in Needs you. Whatever adds a list shows you its fields and any example rows before anything is written, and example rows are marked so you can clear them.

Adding a list to the sidebar and taking it off never touches its rows. A list that leaves the sidebar keeps every row it held.

## Keys on a list

```comparison | Keys in a list
Key | What it does
j and k | Row down and row up, keeping the column
Enter | Open the row
n | New row, with every field
e | Change the cell in place
x | Select, Shift+x to extend, Ctrl+A for all loaded
v | Display options
Delete | Archive, with Put back on the toast
```
